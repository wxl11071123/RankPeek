## Overview

RankPeek is a compact desktop scout for League of Legends. Its visual identity is disciplined and utility-first: a dark graphite workspace, crisp white text, restrained borders, and bright blue actions. The app should feel like a serious performance tool rather than a marketing site. It is dense enough for repeated match review, but it keeps enough empty black canvas around panels for focus.

The default experience is dark. Light mode is an inverted companion for system settings and daylight use, not a separate brand: it keeps the same spacing, typography, blue actions, geometric logo treatment, and sidebar structure while swapping graphite surfaces for white cards on a pale system background.

The product-specific personality comes from two accents. Electric blue marks navigation, primary buttons, charts, and active controls. Muted esports gold is reserved for coaching, rank, fortune, and analysis moments. Gold should feel like a glow from game data, not decoration scattered everywhere.

## Colors

The core palette is black, graphite, white, electric blue, and gold. Use `background` for the app canvas and `surface` for sidebar, title bar, and primary cards. Most panels are separated with subtle outline colors rather than heavy shadows.

Use `primary` for the single most important action in a region: active sidebar item, search button, refresh button, selected tabs, and chart line. `primary-hover` may brighten controls, but avoid making whole surfaces blue.

Use gold only for RankPeek's scouting personality: coach cards, rank badges, analysis glow, and special status feedback. Do not use gold as a general CTA color.

Team and outcome colors are semantic and should stay consistent: blue team uses `team-blue`, red team uses `team-red`, wins and good tags use `success-dark`, losses and bad tags use `error-dark`.

## Typography

Typography is system-like and compact. Inter with Apple/SF fallbacks is the default voice. It should feel native to a desktop utility: sharp, legible, and slightly compressed.

Headings are heavy and direct, usually 24px to 30px. Body copy is mostly 14px with tight line height and a small negative letter spacing. Navigation labels are unusually bold at 17px, which gives the sidebar a confident desktop-app feel.

Use the serif coach typography only inside AI coach and report cards. It gives those panels an "analysis scroll" tone that contrasts with the otherwise clinical interface. Do not spread the serif style into navigation, forms, or normal data cards.

## Layout

The shell uses a fixed desktop frame: a 38px top title bar, a 252px sidebar, and a scrollable content canvas with 24px padding. On narrow viewports the sidebar compresses to icon-only mode and content padding drops to 14px.

Pages are organized as stacked panels with 16px vertical gaps. Most content is left-aligned inside cards; centered empty states are used only when the page has no data yet, such as waiting for a live game.

The home view uses a wider max-width dashboard layout. Settings uses a narrower 720px column to feel like a native preferences panel. Avoid landing-page hero composition, oversized marketing sections, or decorative cards around the entire page.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is mostly tonal. The base canvas is black, primary surfaces sit at graphite, and controls sit one tone higher. Borders are 1px and low contrast; they define edges without making the UI feel boxed-in.

Shadows are functional and dark in the base system. In the home dashboard, gold glow shadows intentionally replace neutral elevation for coach and chart interactions. These glows should appear on hover, focus, or featured analysis surfaces, not on every card.

Backdrop blur belongs to chrome and modal layers. Use it sparingly for sidebar/title-bar polish and expanded coach overlays. Do not turn standard panels into glassmorphism.

## Shapes

The shape language is softly rectangular. Standard controls use 8px radius, cards and panels use 12px to 16px, and large showcase or modal elements can reach 24px to 28px. Pills are reserved for status, tags, switches, and segmented controls.

Rank badges and item slots are tighter than cards: they use 5px to 8px radii and small bevel-like shadows. This preserves the game-data feel while still fitting the wider rounded system.

## Components

Sidebar items are tall, bold, and calm. The active state is a saturated blue rounded rectangle; inactive icons use warm gold, and hover icons can shift toward cyan.

Cards are compact data containers. They should use graphite backgrounds, subtle borders, and 14px to 22px padding. Avoid nested card-on-card decoration unless the inner object is a real data tile, control group, popover, or modal.

Primary buttons are blue, bold, and at least 46px tall in dashboard contexts. Secondary buttons are graphite with borders. Disabled states reduce opacity rather than changing hue.

Inputs and selects use graphite fills and 1px borders. Focus states use the electric blue ring. Filters, tabs, and switches should feel native and compact, never oversized.

Coach and analysis cards are the visual exception. They can use gold borders, smoky dark gradients, serif text, and soft luminous shadows. Expanded coach cards may use more generous padding and stronger blur because they behave like a modal report surface.

Match history cards are denser and more utilitarian than dashboard cards. They may use a dark navy-tinted fill, narrow win/loss rails, champion/item imagery, and compact team rosters.

## Home Dashboard Design Contract

The home dashboard is the reference surface for future tactical pages, including live match information and summoner analysis. New pages should inherit its discipline: neutral module shells, local interaction glow, compact controls, and clear separation between outer containers and internal data UI.

### Module Shells

Top-level page modules use a neutral base state:

- `background: var(--bg-secondary)`
- `border: 1px solid var(--border-color)`
- `box-shadow: none`
- `border-radius: 12px`

Do not add static colored borders, permanent gold/blue glow, or breathing animations to module shells. A module may glow only while hovered, focused, or reached by the existing proximity glow system.

Home module hover color is shared through page-level variables. Dark mode uses muted gold:

- `--home-module-hover-rgb: 212, 175, 55`
- `--home-module-hover-border: rgba(var(--home-module-hover-rgb), 0.48)`
- `--home-module-hover-shadow` with a 1px ring, soft outer glow, and low vertical glow

Light mode uses gray-blue instead of gold or bright blue:

- `--home-module-hover-rgb: 86, 109, 134`
- `--home-module-hover-border: rgba(var(--home-module-hover-rgb), 0.42)`
- `--home-module-hover-shadow` with lower alpha than dark mode

Apply the module hover contract to full module shells such as account, AI analysis, fortune, coach report, and chart cards. Hover/focus must change only the outer border and outer shadow. Avoid inset shadows, large internal tints, radial fills across the module body, and static decorative glows.

### Proximity Glow

RankPeek has two separate glow layers:

- Module glow: whole outer module border and outer shadow on hover/focus.
- Proximity glow: local border light that follows the pointer near a control or surface edge.

Do not merge these into one rule. Keep the existing `surface-glow`, `control-glow`, and `edge-glow` model: JS writes `--control-glow-x`, `--control-glow-y`, and edge alpha variables; CSS decides how that local light is painted.

Controls that are hovered should stack both effects:

- A full border hover color around the entire control.
- A brighter local radial glow near the pointer.

The full border layer should use the hover border token, while the local radial layer should use the local glow token. Do not rely only on a radial gradient for hover, because it makes only the pointer side of the border look active.

Near-glow without hover should stay local and quiet. It should not create a heavy full outer shadow or make the control look selected.

In light mode, module hover borders use gray-blue, but control proximity glow remains gold. The local pointer light should use the light gold edge/core tokens; do not convert proximity glow to gray-blue unless a component has a specific non-home exception.

### Toolbar Controls

Toolbar controls are compact, utility-first, and stable in size. Segmented controls, selects, icon buttons, switches, and filter buttons should keep:

- 1px border.
- 8px to 10px control radius, or pill radius for segmented groups.
- Bold 13px to 14px labels.
- Neutral graphite or white/light-gray fill in the default state.
- No large blue fill unless the control is an active primary or selected state.

For select and segmented filter hover states, use a border-box layered background:

- Padding-box layer: the normal hover fill.
- Border-box layer: radial local glow that fades to the full hover border color.

This gives a complete hover ring and still preserves proximity feedback. Avoid native select pseudo-element tricks if they create inconsistent partial borders.

### Fortune, Coach, and Records

Fortune is a special module but not a blue module. In light mode, fortune surfaces should remain white or neutral gray; slot windows, result areas, and disabled buttons should not use broad blue gradients or blue fills.

Coach and review records may use gold or inherited module glow at the outer container. The internal review card stack is a separate UI object. Do not restyle internal record cards, dots, switching animation, or content layout when only aligning the outer module shell.

### Chart Surface

Charts are data surfaces, not decorative panels. Keep the chart body quiet and readable. Use blue for data lines, active metrics, and focused controls. Use module hover glow only on the chart card shell; do not let the module glow leak into SVG marks, trend points, avatars, badges, or hover badges.

The chart toolbar follows the toolbar control contract above. Queue tabs and selects should look like one family: full hover ring plus local proximity glow, no oversized fill, no layout shift.

### Light Mode

Light mode should be neutral first. Use white, near-white, and cool gray for surfaces. Gray-blue may be used for module hover borders. Bright blue is reserved for primary actions, active controls, and data marks. Avoid light-mode blue washes inside modules unless the specific component is a primary action or active selected control.

### Implementation Checklist

When extending this system to a new page:

- Define page-level module hover variables before writing individual module rules.
- Give every top-level module a neutral base border and `box-shadow: none`.
- Put full module glow only on `:hover` and `:focus-within`.
- Keep proximity glow on controls and edge surfaces through the shared CSS-variable mechanism.
- For hovered controls, layer full border glow and local pointer glow.
- Keep internal data cards, badges, charts, avatars, and buttons separate from outer module shell changes.
- Add focused tests for CSS contracts when changing glow, hover, or light-mode behavior.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the default UI dark, quiet, and data-dense.
- Do use blue for active navigation, primary actions, focus, and charts.
- Do reserve gold for coach, analysis, rank, fortune, and other special scouting moments.
- Do use soft rectangular panels with 1px borders and small tonal shifts.
- Do preserve the fixed desktop-app shell: title bar, sidebar, and scrollable content region.
- Don't turn the app into a marketing page or a full-bleed hero experience.
- Don't use gold as a normal button color.
- Don't add decorative gradients or glow to ordinary settings and list panels.
- Don't mix serif coach typography into standard controls or navigation.
- Don't overcrowd mobile layouts; use the icon sidebar and stacked cards.
